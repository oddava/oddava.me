AniShows is a larger product build than most of my experiments: a real discovery and tracking platform for anime fans.

## Scope

The goal was not just to list shows. I wanted a place where people could:

* track progress
* rate and review titles
* discover new series
* keep up with airing releases

That pushed the project beyond a simple content site into application territory pretty quickly.

## Stack choices

The frontend uses React, while the backend is Django with PostgreSQL as the primary database. Redis and Celery handle the background work and caching that a content-heavy product eventually needs. Docker keeps the local setup consistent across services.

That combination let me move fast on product features without giving up too much structure once the app became more operationally complex.

## What I learned

AniShows taught me that “content product” complexity usually shows up in the data and background tasks first:

* sync jobs
* cache invalidation
* ranking/discovery logic
* image and metadata pipelines

The fun visible part is the UI, but the real work is making the content layer dependable enough that the UI stays useful.
